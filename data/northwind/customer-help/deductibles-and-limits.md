# Fictional Northwind help: Deductibles and limits explained

## Plain definitions

A deductible is the part of a loss that the policyholder bears before any insurance amount may be considered.

A limit is the highest amount that a coverage may consider for a loss, as listed on the policy schedule.

A coverage is a type of protection on a policy, such as collision or comprehensive.

## Illustrative figures

The figures in this article are fictional illustrations only, and the policy page of each policyholder is the authority.

- In an illustration, a collision deductible of $500 is the first $500 of a loss that the policyholder bears.
- In an illustration, a comprehensive deductible of $250 is the first $250 of a loss that the policyholder bears.
- Comprehensive wording may address events other than a collision, such as hail.
- A fictional glass endorsement can describe a $100 glass-only deductible, and that glass-only deductible does not apply to body-panel hail damage.

## Where to find your own deductibles and limits

- Open Policies and choose View schedule and wording for the policy.
- Read the coverage schedule table, which lists each coverage with its limit and its deductible.
- Read the endorsements list, because an endorsement can have its own limit or deductible.

## What a schedule cannot tell you

The schedule alone does not establish coverage for a loss, because the wording, the endorsements, the dates, the facts and a human review also matter.

A human adjuster reviews every claim, and the portal never decides coverage, a settlement amount or payment timing.

The portal does not calculate what a person may receive after a deductible.

Northwind Insurance is a fictional insurer, and this help article about deductibles and limits is a synthetic demonstration, not a coverage decision.
